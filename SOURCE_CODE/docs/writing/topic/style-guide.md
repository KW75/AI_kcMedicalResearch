# Topic Writing Style Guide

## Voice and Tone
- Write in the style of a senior newspaper columnist or editorial writer.
- Use first or third person as appropriate; avoid passive voice.
- Sentence length: aim for 12–20 words on average. Vary rhythm deliberately.
- Open with a hook — a striking fact, a question, or a bold statement.
- Use plain English throughout. No jargon unless immediately explained.
- Avoid hedging language ("it could be argued", "some might say") — take a position.

## Structure
- **Opening paragraph**: hook + thesis statement in no more than 3 sentences.
- **Body**: 3–5 paragraphs each making one clear point with one supporting fact or example.
- **Closing paragraph**: restate key message, end with a call to action or memorable line.
- Use subheadings (##) only if the piece exceeds 1000 words.
- No bullet lists in the body — write in flowing prose.

## Language
- Replace medical jargon with plain equivalents on first use.
- Use active voice: "Doctors prescribe" not "It is prescribed by doctors".
- Avoid hyperbole unless directly supported by data.
- Numbers under ten: spell out. Numbers 10 and above: use numerals.
- Percentages: always use numerals (e.g. 12%).

## Referencing
- Public-facing: hyperlinked author-date citations in parentheses.
- Do not use numbered footnotes — this is not an academic piece.
- Maximum 3–5 citations; choose the strongest evidence only.

## Word Limits
- Standard editorial: 800 words.
- Short commentary: 400 words.
- Long-form feature: 1200 words.
- Do not exceed the agreed limit without explicit instruction.

## Checklist Before Submission
- Hook in opening sentence
- Single clear thesis
- No paragraph longer than 100 words
- Plain language throughout
- Ends with memorable closing line
