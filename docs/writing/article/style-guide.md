# Journal Article Style Guide

## Voice and Tone
- Use formal academic prose throughout.
- Third person preferred; first person permitted in discussion/conclusion.
- Passive voice acceptable in Methods; active voice preferred elsewhere.
- Sentence length: 20–30 words on average. Avoid overly long sentences.
- Avoid emotive or promotional language.

## IMRAD Structure (default for original research)
- **Introduction**: background, gap in knowledge, aims/objectives.
- **Methods**: study design, participants, interventions, outcomes, analysis.
- **Results**: findings only — no interpretation. Use past tense.
- **Discussion**: interpret findings, compare with literature, limitations, implications.
- **Conclusion**: summary of findings and clinical/policy implications.

## Medical Accuracy
- Every clinical claim must cite a peer-reviewed source.
- Spell out drug names in full on first use; generic name first, brand in parentheses.
- State effect sizes and confidence intervals wherever possible, not just p-values.
- Name the study design when describing evidence (e.g. "In a double-blind RCT…").
- Use SI units throughout unless the target journal specifies otherwise.

## Structure and Formatting
- Use ## for main section headings (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion).
- Use ### for subsections within each section.
- Each paragraph opens with a topic sentence.
- Figures and tables referenced in the body before they appear.
- Abstract: structured (Background, Methods, Results, Conclusion) ≤ 250 words.

## Referencing
- Vancouver numbered style by default.
- All in-text citations use superscript numbers [1].
- Reference list at end, numbered in order of appearance.
- DOIs included where available.

## Word Limits
- Original research article: 3500 words (excluding abstract and references).
- Review article: 5000 words.
- Case report: 1500 words.
- Abstract: 250 words.
- Do not exceed the agreed limit without explicit instruction.
